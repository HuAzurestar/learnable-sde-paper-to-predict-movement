\begin{abstract}
We investigate bounded nonlinear acceleration residuals in a kinetic stochastic differential equation (SDE), separating statistical recovery, development-set forecasting, and differentiable-objective feasibility. Two basis families complete their registered synthetic experiments but each recovers only 12 of 20 teacher-matched settings under the unchanged joint recovery criterion. A seven-candidate real-data comparison yields 63 measured cells per model out of 282 declared cells. Whole-unit eligibility remains one; the reported 6/6/5-unit horizon comparisons are exposed, coverage-conditional exploration, not a qualified superiority test. Mean Energy-score differences favor the nonlinear model on covered cells, but calibration is poor and its 60-second mean endpoint error is worse. Six authored path-objective fits and twelve paired gradient-route checks pass bounded feasibility criteria, without demonstrating optimization convergence or general speed or memory advantages. Thus the study establishes limited implementation feasibility, not a globally superior forecasting model or a qualified latent-state method.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}
Adding nonlinear acceleration to a movement model can increase representation without establishing identifiability, calibrated uncertainty or long-horizon predictive value. A useful study must distinguish a failed qualification from an unexecuted experiment and a completed computation from a favorable scientific outcome. We extend the project's affine kinetic-SDE implementation with constrained residual families and differentiable-objective checks. This report \emph{extends} that engineering baseline; it neither replicates an external paper's empirical population nor supersedes the repository's separate SAR manuscript. Historical failed/interrupted attempts remain visible with their causes and costs.

\section{Related work}\label{sec:related}
Proper scoring rules provide distribution-sensitive forecast evaluation~\cite{gneiting2007}; finite ensembles still have Monte Carlo variation. Stochastic adjoints~\cite{li2020} and reversible solvers~\cite{kidger2021} motivate gradient routes but do not guarantee an advantage on every finite CPU problem. SDE matching~\cite{bartosh2025} motivates examining marginal/path-law compatibility, not assuming an arbitrary flow satisfies the fixed kinetic position equation. We evaluate our registered cases rather than claim replication or contradiction of those papers' general empirical findings.

\section{Questions and claim boundary}\label{sec:questions}
RQ1 asks whether constrained basis residuals recover a known discrete teacher under a fixed design. RQ2 asks whether the existing nonlinear fit improves forecasting over its affine comparator on the registered development population. RQ3 asks whether a trajectory-distribution objective and alternative gradient routes can execute correctly within explicit small-case resource limits. These are different questions: neither engineering tests nor synthetic feasibility qualify a real-data ranking. The final test set is not read. This report does not make an equivalence, formal NO\_GAIN, population superiority, or deployment-readiness claim.

\section{Model and objectives}\label{sec:model}
For planar position $p$ and velocity $v$, the observed-state model is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:model}
 dp_t=v_t\,dt,\qquad
 dv_t=[A v_t+B p_t+a+r_\theta(p_t,v_t,c_t)]\,dt+L\,dW_t.
\end{equation}
Context $c$ is absent in the authored fixtures. The position equation is fixed; the diffusion is constant and rank two in the four-dimensional state. M0 sets $r=0$. M1-S uses cubic splines; M1-R uses Gaussian radial basis functions. M2 uses a bounded neural residual with two width-16 hidden layers, spectral bounds and an amplitude bound. Real fits and their normalizer are reused, not retrained in response to evaluation. The authored neural fixtures use identity normalization, a unit spectral bound, unit amplitude and residual scale one; these fixture settings must not be mistaken for newly selected real fits.

For observed increments, O1 estimates acceleration after accounting for the affine component. For constant nonsingular velocity noise and known affine terms, a synthetic one-step response has the form
\begin{equation}\label{eq:noise}
 Y_i=r_\theta(z_i)+L\xi_i/\sqrt{\Delta t},\qquad \xi_i\sim N(0,I).
\end{equation}
The zero-penalty full-rank least-squares covariance is proportional to
$(\Phi^\top\Phi)^{-1}\otimes LL^\top/\Delta t$, with the factor ordering depending on vectorization. At fixed design, repetitions $N$ give a $1/(N\Delta t)$ scale. Smaller steps with fixed $N$ therefore do not guarantee more drift information. This analytic interpretation is post hoc, not a new empirical test, a criterion change, or a unique causal explanation of the observed recovery failures.

O2 averages forecast-distribution scores over fixed training origins and horizons. For $K>1$ simulated positions $X_k$ and target $y$, the implemented unbiased pair estimator is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:energy}
 \widehat{\mathrm{ES}}=\frac1K\sum_k\|X_k-y\|_2
 -\frac{1}{2K(K-1)}\sum_{k\ne l}\|X_k-X_l\|_2.
\end{equation}
The Energy score evaluates a predictive distribution rather than just its mean~\cite{gneiting2007}. The estimator identity does not by itself prove the paths are calibrated or the fitted model adequate. Endpoint distance here uses the ensemble-mean position, not a selected best path.

\section{RQ3: limited objective and gradient feasibility}\label{sec:rq3}
\subsection{G1: direct path-objective fitting}
Three authored seeds (26101411--26101413) each use steps 0.25/0.125 seconds, for six cells. Source O1 preparation uses 512 one-step transitions per seed and 40 actual updates. The teacher and student share the documented constrained architecture; the teacher is not fitted from real data. Each O2 fit uses eight paths, two training origins, horizons 10/30 seconds, twelve maximum updates, learning rate 0.005, gradient clipping 100 and a six-step horizon curriculum. Two additional authored challenge origins are never used for checkpoint selection. The restored checkpoint is best on the fixed independent-Brownian training monitor over all training origins and horizons, not necessarily the final iterate.
\tabfile{o2-feasibility.tex}
All six cells pass their predeclared small-case feasibility gate: valid paths, four central finite differences agreeing with autograd (step $10^{-5}$, absolute tolerance $10^{-5}$, relative tolerance $10^{-3}$), fit-only wall time at most 600 seconds and lifetime peak RSS at most 2 GiB. Every fit stops with \texttt{MAX\_STEPS}; this is not convergence. The 600-second/2-GiB gates qualify measurements rather than impose additional research termination caps. All 24 paired authored challenge score rows are retained in the safe summary: some improve and some worsen. Training grids are not pathwise coupled, so cross-grid differences are neither an isolated discretization experiment nor a convergence proof. O1 and O2 losses are different objectives and are not ranked against each other.

\subsection{G2 and G3: matched forward problems}
We evaluate the same fixed O1-prepared checkpoints, without model updates, at the three seeds and two grids. G2 pairs direct reversible Heun with its reversible adjoint; G3 pairs direct midpoint with a continuous-adjoint midpoint route. The routes are motivated by stochastic adjoint and reversible-solver work~\cite{li2020,kidger2021}, but qualification here concerns only the actual installed implementation and these twelve cases. Forward solvers are matched \emph{within} pairs; cross-solver differences are not isolated gradient-method effects. State-independent diffusion makes the It\^o and Stratonovich drifts coincide in these fixtures.

All routes share a halfway-tree Brownian construction, fixed entropy per seed, tolerance $10^{-9}$, and explicitly checked coarse increments equal to sums of fine increments. Each pair uses three fresh sequential helpers: finite-difference verifier, direct route and candidate route. Benchmark order is balanced. All nonempty acceleration parameters enter the full-gradient comparison; zero-size context tensors do not. Four finite-difference coordinates are checked per pair. Full-gradient relative $\ell_2$ tolerances are $10^{-3}$ for G2 and 0.05 for G3, with separate coordinate, forward-agreement and resource gates in the recipe.
\tabfile{gradient-routes.tex}
All twelve pairs pass; paired forward differences are zero. Maximum relative gradient discrepancies are about $2.801\times10^{-14}$ (G2) and $3.323\times10^{-4}$ (G3). Every candidate is slower than its matched direct route in these small CPU cases. RSS is a fresh-process lifetime peak including imports and metadata, not phase-isolated working memory; no general memory advantage, asymptotic comparison, trained-O2 result or real-data speedup is claimed.

\section{Latent and matrix-method disposition}\label{sec:latent}
The current contract supplies observed states but no observation likelihood, noise calibration, latent initial law, recognition model or identifiability conditions. Latent-state estimation is therefore not qualified or empirically assessed, not shown universally unnecessary. With $G=[0;L]$, a valid Girsanov drift change must lie in $\mathrm{range}(G)$ and preserve $dp=v\,dt$; adding an inverse or fictitious position noise is invalid. A generic Gaussian marginal flow can violate the fixed position drift, so it cannot simply be substituted as a path-law construction. This is a counterexample to that substitution, not to every latent-flow approach~\cite{bartosh2025}.

Instantaneous rank two does not imply a singular finite-time transition. For affine $F=[0,I;B,A]$ and nonsingular $L$, the controllability blocks
\[
 [G,FG]=\begin{bmatrix}0&L\\L&AL\end{bmatrix}
\]
have rank four; the positive-time covariance Gramian can be nonsingular. Exact affine joint transitions remain legitimate, unlike inversion of a singular four-dimensional Euler covariance. Latent components L1/L2 and matrix-gradient route G4 remain \texttt{NOT\_ACTIVATED} under this contract; there are no fabricated ELBO, latent-matching or G4 benchmark values. G3 requires no diffusion inverse and is not reclassified as an unrun latent experiment.

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}
RQ1 fails the unchanged all-settings matched-recovery criterion. RQ2 remains inconclusive because the registered whole-unit comparison lacks population eligibility, the exposed partial comparison is coverage-conditional, and calibration is poor. RQ3 establishes six direct-objective and twelve matched-route small-case feasibility passes, not convergence, a universal adjoint advantage or a latent-state result. The defensible outcome is a bounded negative/feasibility report with explicit missingness and retained failure history, not selection of a globally superior movement predictor.
